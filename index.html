Cycle monitor settings

The iCOMM cloud doesn't say when the heater is running. For accurate on/off cycles, point this at a power reading for the heater's circuit. Without one, cycles are worked out from the iCOMM kWh counter, which is much coarser.

  • Shelly Pro EM / EM Gen3: http://IP/rpc/EM1.GetStatus?id=0, field act_power
  • Shelly Pro 3EM: http://IP/rpc/EM.GetStatus?id=0, field total_act_power
  • Shelly EM (Gen1): http://IP/status, field emeters.0.power
  • Home Assistant: http://HA:8123/api/states/sensor.water_heater_power, field state, plus a long-lived access token
  • Tasmota: http://IP/cm?cmnd=Status%208, field StatusSNS.ENERGY.Power

The heat pump draws a few hundred watts and the elements about 4,500 W, so "Element above" tells them apart. COP is how much heat the heat pump moves per unit of electricity (about 3; lower in a cold room). Changes apply from the next reading.

    Solar settings

    Best: point this at your solar server (the EG4 solar dashboard). When it reads the inverter's Wi-Fi dongle, readings arrive every few seconds and the inverter cut-off reacts within seconds. If the solar server can't be reached, this reads the EG4 Monitor account below instead, about once a minute. Your EG4 password is saved on this server, readable only by the app.

    The water heater is on the inverter's output, so the element (about 4,500 W) counts against the inverter's rating. Electric-mode dumping only starts when the house load plus 4,500 W stays under the cut-off. If the output reaches the cut-off while dumping, the heater goes back to its previous mode right away. With the solar server reading the dongle, the cut-off is checked every 5 seconds; from the EG4 cloud the data is about a minute old. Either way this backs up the inverter's own overload protection; it doesn't replace it.
